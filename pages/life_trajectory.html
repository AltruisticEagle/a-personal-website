A bit about me

A long thesis about how I came to be who I am

I - Who am I? Where did I come from?

My name is Jason. I'm a high school student. The thing with me is that, although I feel like it isn't obvious for me, I'm an immigrant. I was born on a summer day some time ago (but I am not about to go around telling strangers when I was born), and I came to this wonderful country called Canada on another warm summer day some other time ago, just under halfway through my life so far, more specifically to a city called Toronto, where most my memories are contained. I like this country. It's pretty flat and big; from what I've seen of my adventures through North America, Toronto is the most charming "flat and big" place I know. America is too big for me to like. Go Canada. I love this place so much.

author's note: I like summer because I can be nostalgic. However, I can't say I "like" summer because it can get too warm. Also, I think I can be better dressed in winter; shorts and a t-shirt aren't good-looking, to quote a friend I met in Vancouver.

II - My first moments in this country

At the risk of oversharing, I'll tell you about how my parents chose to take this incredible challenge on to get us over here.

I inherited this fighting spirit from them. I fight for stuff. Without this spirit, I would not have tried to do the whole of Stardance Challenge's 30 hour certificate in a week as I am now.

My parents are pretty smart people, to say the least. So they recognized that I can get a better life here in the True North. And this is how they descended down the laundry list of countries in the West. It was actually pretty funny.

  • UK: too expensive.
  • Germany: good, but German is a small language and I wouldn't benefit from learning it.
  • USA: We actually don't really know why we didn't move to the US. I think it might have been the '16 election, actually.
  • France: the Frenchmen aren't known for their... tolerance of non-Francophones, and my parents didn't speak French.
  • Spain: I don't remember why! I think it was actually pretty high on their list. I blame Mariano Rajoy.
  • Canada: I think it was because it's an immigrant country. It's a good place, for sure.

So we chose Canada. O Canada, our home and native land...
True patriot love... in all of us command!
Car ton bras sait porter l'épée, il sait porter la croix!
Ton histoire est une épopée, des plus brillants exploits!
God keep our land... glorious and free!
O Canada... We stand on guard, for, thee~
O Canada, ! We stand on GUARD, FOR, THEEEEEE!

I typed the above, not copy pasted. Honestly, this anthem is goated.

I remembered that I enrolled in school somewhat late, maybe only in October of 2017. In our defense, it doesn't really matter because it's Grade 2. Hopefully the Ivys don't really care that I missed the first month or so of Grade 2. Besides, we only arrived in early/mid August, which doesn't offer us the biggest possible leeway to get me enrolled. I don't remember too much about the school, to be perfectly honest. I remembered that I knew how to speak English, for better or for worse. That was a good thing.

Memory Snippet 1

Somehow, one of the more cherished memories I have of first coming to Canada is the Lawrence Mall. I remember that there was a Fortino's supermarket inside of it. So somehow that became a core memory. I also remember that we discovered that apparently we can slice bread in that supermarket and buy frozen food. Again. We're immigrants. There's something so bizarre about remembering this so clearly in my head. I also remember, for some damned reason, that we first slept on some very low-quality rubber air mattresses that smelled like burnt tires.

Memory Snippet 2

So this is where I put on the tinfoil hat and psychoanalyse 7-year-old me, and say I'm probably not exactly neurotypical. We bought a huge marble set - you know, marble tracks where marbles can trickle down. Apparently I didn't really like how I only get 20 shitty plastic marbles, so my dad brought a couple hundred from China the next time he came to visit us. I remember that I also bawled my eyes out for a couple hours after dropping some of them down the stairs, which made me incredibly upset because I thought I was going to lose a lot of them. Actually, I still worry about that today, because I don't remember where exactly in the attic I store the marbles long-term. Maybe I should look into that. I loved building large tracks. Perhaps an engineering trait.

Memory Snippet 3

I used to have this really cool bookroom on the second floor of our house. Actually, I still miss it. I actually really wanted to put my bed in there, but unfortunately that wasn't possible. I spent loads of time in there doing fun things, especially reading. I have a 1993 version of the World Book Encyclopedia that we picked up for free from a household that were about to throw it away, or just leave it there. I remember that I saw the manual transmission article, and 9-year-old me immediately went like "mom please let's carry this back into the car I want to bring this home". Hmm, I think the universe has been dropping hints of engineering in my life before I knew what that is.

Memory Snippet 4

There were two important places with the word "park" in their names in my early Canadian misadventures. The first place is called Dell Park. There's nothing really that special about it, it was just a park near where we lived before we moved, and it was the first park I ever visited in Canada. I had so much fun there. It's still there, so far as I'm aware. I played with that periscope-looking thing that can transmit sound all across the grounds to the other pipe. Still am amazed by them.

Memory Snippet 5

The second place is called Glen Park. It was an elementary school I went to for one school year, G2, before I got my place in TFS, and it wasn't too far down the street from Dell Park. I used to walk there - that was the only time where I could walk to school, and my mom made me recite the times table on the way there. Well. Another math/STEM hint from the universe somehow. I can't possibly make this up.

IV - That sad, sad time where the whole world shut down due to a lot of bad germs from bats

RIP, every death from COVID, first of all. As a 10-year-old, it was shocking to see millions die. Away from their families, their livelihoods taken away by a virus.

COVID was such a strange time for me. I was in China for the first time in three whole years. I barely even remember the country. But there I was.

It was a weird time for so many reasons. I didn't have any friends back in China at all, during the whole Grade 5 year. Mayne that lack of social interaction there had lingering effects of some kind.

Memory Snippet 1

The first thing I did after I got off of the plane and set up the VPN was to check the 2020 election, actually. I wasn't as much of a politics nerd back then, but I saw the blue on the map being on the winner's side. I also remember how Biden's electoral vote count was stuck at 264 or something for the longest time, until it finally ticked over. I also remember the two Georgia US Senate seats also being up fo grabs.

Memory Snippet 2

I've since stopped doing hockey, but a bit part of the sport I did back then was hockey. Come to think of it, I recall that my mom was talking about how a popular sport that is very accessible back in Canada has suddenly became somewhat of an upper-class priviledge when I was trying to find equipment and coaches in China.

V - Coming back from a 20-month stint away from everyone I know, and all the time up until now

Coming back was... strange. A really funny thing was when I came back for the first time, I stepped into Pearson airport, and... I wasn't sure what was going on. At that time, that was it! 20 months' overstay in my foreign home country, and it was just suddenly over. I remember that like it happened yesterday. Honestly, the rest has been a blur. G7. G8. G9. G10. And now G11, starting in just half a week. It's all been a blur, honestly.
